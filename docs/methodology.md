# Methodology v1 — Chokepoint scoring

Four sub-scores, 0–100, each assigned from documented evidence, then weighted.

| Sub-score | Weight | What it measures | Evidence type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trade | 40% | share of global seaborne value/volume that can be disrupted | EIA chokepoint data, UNCTAD Review of Maritime Transport |
| Military | 30% | ability of a state/actor to interdict the passage | naval presence, coastal control, AIS incident history |
| Lack of alternatives | 20% | cost/time penalty of the shortest viable detour | route distance, canal draft limits, pipeline capacity |
| Fragility | 10% | probability of closure (conflict, drought, grounding) | conflict proximity, single-point failures |

Final score = round(0.4·T + 0.3·M + 0.2·A + 0.1·F)

**Known limitations (stated on screen, never hidden):**
- Sub-scores are editorial judgements anchored to public data, not a statistical index.
- Weights are a choice; a different weighting changes the ranking of the middle five.
- The top two (Hormuz, Malacca) are robust to any reasonable reweighting.
