Say you're a photographer on an Antarctic assignment, and you've just watched a leopard seal kill a penguin beside your boat. The water is minus one degrees Celsius. Your guide says get in with your camera. You do, numb with fear. The seal is about 3.7 meters long and weighs around 450 kilograms.
She rushes you and takes your camera, and your head, into her mouth. It looks like a threat display, and it lasts a few minutes. Then she relaxes, catches a penguin, stops about three meters away, and lets it go. It swims toward you. You can't catch it, so she tries again.
When live penguins fail, she brings dead ones. Four or five float around your head. She flips them onto you, pushes you around, blows bubbles in your face, and tries to force-feed your camera. On the last day, a second leopard seal swims in. She chases it off, takes its penguin, and brings it to you.
Over four days, Paul Nicklen decided she'd concluded that he was a useless predator who was going to starve in her ocean. Honestly, fair.
