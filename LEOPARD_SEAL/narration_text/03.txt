Start with what's on the menu. Anything small enough to kill. Krill is the winter staple, then fish, squid, penguins, and the pups of other seals. Seven penguin species are on that list, from chinstraps to emperors. One leopard seal caught near Sydney had eaten a fully grown platypus, which says two things: leopard seals wander, and they're not picky.
If you're a young crabeater seal, the odds are grim. About seventy-eight percent of crabeaters carry parallel scars consistent with escaping a leopard seal. And if you're a newly fledged penguin, you're the most vulnerable bird on the menu, because seals ambush you on the way to and from the colony.
Each seal hunts her own way, too. In 2013 and 2014, researchers strapped cameras to seven females at Livingston Island and collected more than fifty hours of footage. Scat studies had suggested the seals fed mostly on krill offshore. The video showed them staying near the coast, hunting fur seal pups and fish. Some stalked fish and flushed them from crevices. Others chased them in open water. And the smallest seal in the study had her meal stolen by another leopard seal six times. Six. Even the top of the food chain gets its lunch taken.
