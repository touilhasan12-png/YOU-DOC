First, the hardware. The leopard seal is the largest of the four Antarctic seal species, and the biggest females pass three and a half meters and five hundred kilograms. Its jaws are wide and massive, with long curving canines for gripping, and back teeth with three interlocking cusps that strain krill out of the water. A murder weapon with a colander built in. In short bursts it can reach forty kilometers an hour, and its only natural predator is the orca. That says plenty about the neighborhood. Leopard seals live more than twenty-six years, mostly alone, scattered across millions of square miles of pack ice, which makes them hard to study and harder to predict.
But the hardware isn't what makes it dangerous. The curiosity is.
