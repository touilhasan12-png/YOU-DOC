Suppose a leopard seal is circling you in the water. As Nicklen put it, you're at the predator's mercy. Your options?
Swim away? It reaches forty kilometers an hour, and the researchers list moving rapidly away and turning away among the things that can make a seal react more. Block its path? Also on the list. Blow bubbles at it? Also on the list. Fight? Gareth Wood's friends did, with crampons. Nobody recommends it.
Which leaves the dullest advice in Antarctica: stay calm, keep facing the seal, and if things escalate, retreat slowly, still facing it. After 2003, the British Antarctic Survey added a thirty-minute watch for marine mammals before any dive, and banned snorkeling. If one turns up on a beach, New Zealand's Department of Conservation says stay at least twenty meters away and never touch or feed it. The best defense against a leopard seal is not being in its water in the first place.
