Leopard seals are ambush hunters that feed in the water, not on land. Now say you're the penguin. The seal catches the penguin in the water, then brings its prey to the surface to feed.
The best place for this is the ice edge, where penguins bunch up before they dive. In one study at Prydz Bay, seals worked the fast-ice edge where departing Adélie penguins gathered. Researchers saw five different hunting techniques and estimated that just six seals could eat about two point seven percent of the adult Adélie population over roughly 120 days.
Now look at who else stands at the ice edge. Penguins stand upright. So do you. The researchers' best idea is that against white ice, you look like a dark, upright shape, and the only other thing that stands up out there is a penguin.
