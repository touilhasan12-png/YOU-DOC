So how would you read one? You can't, really. When researchers listed what leopard seals do around people in the water, the same pattern kept coming up. The seal approaches, often from above. It circles in closer. It disappears from view, then reappears. It lifts its head, shakes it, and opens and shuts its mouth. Many observers described that as curious. Nicklen got a few of those, and a penguin.
But the authors also flagged the possible warning signs: circling in toward the person, approaching the head, and blowing air or bubbles. And that open mouth? Many people read it as aggression, and between leopard seals it's part of aggressive and defensive displays. In other seals, an open mouth is often a sign of submission. It might also mean the animal feels threatened, and a threatened animal can be unpredictable. Boyd's own summary was that leopard seals are inquisitive about people, probably sizing them up as potential prey. So the same display can mean curious, scared, or deciding. Which is exactly what a test looks like from the inside.
