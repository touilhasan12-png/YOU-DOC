So what happens when you're the dark shape? British Antarctic Survey researchers collected questionnaires from seventy people describing 137 encounters with leopard seals. The majority of attacks happened at the ice edge, and in most of them the seal wasn't seen beforehand. In more than half the reports from sea ice, the seal pursued the person. In 1985, a Scottish polar explorer named Gareth Wood was bitten on the leg by a seal that broke through thin ice. His companions kicked it in the head with ice crampons until it let go. That's Antarctic first aid.
If you're thinking a boat is safer, the seals disagree. The same study counted thirteen cases of physical contact with small boats, including punctured rubber ones, and American researchers have had to fit guards on their inflatables to stop the seals from popping them.
