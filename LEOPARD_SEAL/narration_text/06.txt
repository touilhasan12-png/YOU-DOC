Then there's the one that changed everything. On July 22, 2003, Kirsty Brown, a 28-year-old marine biologist with the British Antarctic Survey, was snorkeling with her partner twenty meters from shore at Rothera research station, studying how icebergs scour marine life. A leopard seal held her underwater. Her colleagues pulled her out, and the station doctor tried to revive her for about an hour. She was pronounced dead at 16:50.
It remains the only documented fatal leopard seal attack, and at the time the British Antarctic Survey called it completely unprecedented. The coroner recorded accidental death by drowning caused by a leopard seal attack. The seal was estimated at four and a half meters long, measured against the rescue boat, and the study's authors concluded it was hunting, not being curious. Ian Boyd, then director of the Sea Mammal Research Unit, offered three explanations: it was startled and defended itself, it mistook her for another seal, or it knew exactly what she was. And neither the shore party nor her partner saw it coming. Which means "just watch for it" isn't a plan.
